# Attribute claims

Go through a draft one claim at a time. The aim is that every factual statement either has a source the audience can check, or is worded so it does not claim more than the source supports.

## Method

1. **List every checkable claim.** Numbers, dates, names, places, quotes, rankings ("largest", "first"), causes ("because", "led to"), and anything stated about what a person or organisation did or intended. One row per claim. Opinions and questions are not claims.
2. **Find the best available source for each.** Prefer in this order: primary records (court orders, filings, official data, parliamentary replies, the original post or video), then first-hand reporting by a named outlet, then secondary summaries. Open the source. Do not rely on memory or on a search snippet.
3. **Compare wording, not gist.** Check the number, the unit, the date, who said it, and what exactly they said it about. Most errors are a correct fact attached to the wrong year, place or speaker.
4. **Give each claim a verdict.**

   | Verdict | Meaning | What to do |
   |---|---|---|
   | OK | The source says this | Keep. Record the source. |
   | FIX | The source says something different | Give the corrected wording. |
   | SOFTEN | The source supports a weaker statement | Give wording that matches the source. |
   | UNVERIFIED | No source found, or it could not be opened | Cut it, or say on the record that it could not be confirmed. |

5. **Apply the attribution rules** below, then return the table.

## Attribution rules

- **Say who says it before saying what they say** when a fact is contested: "The state government says 40 families were moved. Residents told reporters the number was over 200."
- **An allegation stays an allegation** until a court or the party itself confirms it. Use "alleged", "according to", "is reported to have". Never turn "X is accused of" into "X did".
- **Give the other side's stated position** wherever a person or organisation is accused of something. If they did not respond or no response is on record, say that.
- **Do not claim a cover-up, motive or intent** without direct evidence of it. Describe what the records show and what they do not.
- **Projections are not facts.** "Is expected to reach", "the company plans", with the source and the date of the projection.
- **Official averages can hide the event.** If a headline figure is a state-wide or annual average, check whether it describes the specific place and time in the draft.
- **Date every moving number** (tolls, counts, prices): "as of 10 August".
- **Private individuals:** no names, faces, addresses or identifying details unless they are already central to published reporting and naming them serves the story. When unsure, leave them out.
- **Check the superlatives** ("biggest", "first ever", "never before"). They are wrong more often than any other kind of claim.

## Output

A table with columns: number, claim as written, verdict, source (with link and date), suggested wording. Then three short lists: claims that need a response from the other side, claims that will go out of date (with the date to recheck), and anything you could not open or confirm.

Report honestly. If half the draft is UNVERIFIED, say so. Do not mark a claim OK because it sounds right or because several sites repeat it; repeated claims often trace back to one unchecked original.
